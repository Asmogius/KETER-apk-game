# 1. Introduction & Setting

You are an officer aboard the Reconnaissance Vessel Thoth. Your mission: investigate the loss of communication with the Mars research colony KETER. The docking bay is in shambles when your crew boards — you didn't expect a warm welcome, but you expected something. Your commander has orders for you to prepare a Strike Team for deployment. You're going in blind. Casualties are expected.

## Quests

As you traverse the board, you will cross paths with surviving occupants of KETER. Some may ask for your help; some may even pay you for it. These are issued as Quest Cards, which are turned in at the Hangar for their specified reward once their objectives are complete.

## How to Progress

Your team grows in power as you collect more cards, level up, collect experimental Prototypes, recruit more powerful Heroes, and expand your understanding of the newly discovered element Aetherite. Player Level increases by 1 each time a Black Lab is cleared, which in turn unlocks higher-Level Loot.

## How to Win

Discover and clear each District's Black Lab to gain access to the Nexus — the headquarters of the executive of all six Megacorps. Entering the Nexus triggers the final boss fight.

## Game Modes (Work in Progress)

- Full Game

- Arcade (quick game)

- PvP

- Boss Rush

The World Map is the entire Research Colony KETER — a massive structure on Mars, completely enclosed against the harsh atmochip. KETER is split into six Districts, each managed by one of the six all-powerful Megacorps that govern Humanity, and each with its own specialized field of research. Each District also has its own variant of the newly discovered Aether Energy.

## How Affinity Is Used

Affinity means something slightly different depending on the card type:

- **Hero Cards — **determines which District the Hero is connected to. Card mechanics and themes are tied to that District's technology and often synergize with it.

- **Enemy Cards — **each Enemy has an Affinity to 1 of the 6 District fields of research. Enemies typically only appear in their correlating District.

- **Combat Cards and Action Cards — **any card of any Affinity may be taken into combat, but a card that costs District Aether can only be paid for with Aether of that District. Bring the matching Aether Chips, or the card is dead weight. The Aether Chip is the only gate: no Hero, Troop or Combat Role needs to match a card's Affinity to use it.

These abbreviations appear on card stat bars in place of full stat names.

| INT | Integrity | Armour against Cyber, 1 to 20: a hostile Cyber Action must Penetrate it, as an attack must Armor Class. | All |

Every Unit carries Integrity. Heroes also carry Leadership. (Revision 15.5: Integrity replaced Will and Security.)

## Subtypes

Revision 15.5. Every Unit — Hero, Troop and Enemy alike — has **one or two subtypes**: Melee, Ranged, Support or Cyber. A Unit's subtypes decide **which kinds of Actions it can have**. Subtypes were once Human, Mechanical or Cyborg; what a Unit is made of is now lore and flavour only, and no rule reads it.

Subtypes are displayed as icons to the left of the card name, in the top-left corner, separate from Affinity in the name bar corner.

| **Subtype** | **Icon** | **Meaning** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Melee | Fist | Fights at close quarters. |
| Ranged | Crosshair | Attacks at a distance. |
| Support | Cross | Buffs, heals, and controls rather than dealing direct damage. |
| Cyber | Processor | Hacks: its Actions must Penetrate Integrity rather than Armor Class. |

### What Subtypes Do

- **Decide a Unit's Actions.** A Unit can have only Actions of its own subtypes. **Passives are exempt** (Revision 15.6): a Passive applies whatever the Unit's subtypes.

- **Enable targeting.** A subtype is a narrowing word (Revision 15.6): written after a Target it narrows it — *target Ranged ally*, *all Melee enemies within 2 tiles*. See Targets and Narrowing, Section 9.

# 3. Heroes

A Hero is a type of Friendly Unit. Heroes have undergone gene-editing experimental therapy to infuse Aetherite Energy with their DNA, allowing them to directly harness Aether Energy for various effects and varying effectiveness. Hero Cards are generally stronger than Troop Cards. A Strike Team holds up to 3 Heroes, who share one Deck; the Heroes entering a Combat begin on the Battlefield, and one of them leads it.

## Hero Traits

**Affinity: **Some Heroes have a certain knack for the mysterious technology found throughout KETER. They have abilities ordinary Troops lack, and as they gain experience, that power grows.

**Passive Abilities: **Heroes often inspire their teams, and this can have persistent effects on the Battlefield.

**Active Abilities: **Heroes often perform superhuman feats, but these take a toll and cannot be performed too often.

**Progression: **As the Player reaches a higher Level, they gain access to higher-Level Cards. Higher-Level Cards can be used regardless of Player Level, but Loot generally matches Player Level.

## Hero Stats

| **Stat** | **Abbrev.** | **Description** | **Typical Range** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health Points | HP | How much damage a Unit can take before being Incapacitated. | Min 15 / Max 60 |
| Armor Class | AC | Reduces the likelihood an attack deals damage. See Section 6. | Min 5 / Max 16 |
| Action Slots | +⚔ | Number of Action Cards assigned to the Hero before combat. | Min 1 / Max 10 |
| Energy per Turn | +NRG | NRG generated each turn by and for that Hero; unspent NRG carries over. | Min 0 / Max 6 |
| Movement Speed | MS | Tiles a Hero can move in one Combat turn. | Min 1 / Max 6 |
| Leadership | LDR | Unit Slots for Troops that start combat alongside the Hero, at a 1:1 ratio. | Min 1 / Max 30 |

Every Hero also carries a Combat Role — Melee, Ranged, Support, or any combination — which describes how they fight. It does not limit the cards they may use. See Section 2.

Heroes begin combat with 0 Energy by default. Any Hero that starts with a different amount states so directly on their card.

Damage is not a Unit stat. It is attached to individual Actions, so a Unit's damage output depends entirely on the Action it uses.

## Leadership & Unit Slots

Leadership sets how many Troops **start the combat** alongside the Hero, at a 1:1 ratio of Unit Slots — a Hero with Leadership 8 may begin a combat with Troops occupying up to 8 Unit Slots already on the Battlefield.

Starting Troops are chosen from the Strike Team's Deck before the combat begins. They leave the Deck and are placed on Player Spawn Tiles at no Aether cost. When several Heroes enter a combat, their Leadership is added together into one pool.

Leadership limits only the Troops that start the combat. Troops played from the Deck during combat have no cap. See Section 4.

## Energy & Aether

These are two separate resources and are never interchangeable:

- **Energy (NRG) — **generated by the Hero. Used to perform Hero Actions, which have varied NRG costs. Unspent NRG carries over to the next turn.

- **Aether — **generated by Aether Chips in the Aether Bank and by Heroes. Used to play Combat Cards, and by any Action Card whose use cost includes Aether. Unspent Aether is lost at the end of the turn unless a card states otherwise.

- Some District 3 Heroes can Overclock, immediately gaining bonus NRG in exchange for either HP loss or gaining Drained X. Overclock is a Status Effect. (Revision 16.0: Overclock has no rules in a fight yet; see Modifiers, Section 9.)

- Some Hero Actions can be stacked multiple times by paying extra, similar to Magic: The Gathering.

**Drained X: **the Unit has half Movement Speed for X turns.

Heroes have between 1 and 3 preset Actions or Passives; the rest come from Action Cards assigned to their Action Slots. Stronger or higher-Affinity Heroes have more Action Slots.

## Action Cards

Action Cards are **never part of the Deck.** They are assigned to Heroes before a combat, up to each Hero's Action Slots, and are carried into the combat by that Hero. A Hero may go in with empty Action Slots.

An assigned Action is used like one of the Hero's own printed Actions, as many times as it can be paid for. Each Action Card prints its **use cost**: Energy, Aether, or both. An Action whose use cost includes District Aether cannot be used without Aether of that District in the Aether Bank.

Assignments are part of the Strike Team and are kept between combats, but may be changed before any combat — including during a deployment, since Action Cards are not part of the Deck and the Access Lock does not cover them.

## World Map Movement Speed

World Map Movement Speed is not printed on the Hero card — it is calculated from the Roster. Take the Movement Speed of every Hero being deployed, exclude any value of 0, average them, and round up to the nearest whole number. Troops are excluded from this calculation. Only 1 unexplored tile can be traveled to per turn regardless of this value.

## Origins

Whether a Unit is Human, Cyborg or Mechanical is lore and flavour only (Revision 15.5); no rule reads it. **Every Troop is a mechanical robot** — a crafted soldier would not fit the world — while Heroes and Enemies may be of any origin.

## Hero & Troop Archetypes

Archetypes are an official game mechanic. An Archetype is a label attached to Hero, Troop, and Enemy Cards that other card effects can reference — for example, "when this Card enters the Battlefield, deal 5 Damage to all Assassin Units on the Battlefield," or "all Infantry Troops gain +1 Movement Speed until end of turn." Archetype labels are printed on the card and are always active for targeting purposes.

- Juggernaut — high HP/AC, low Damage/Movement. Mostly Melee; stronger versions can be Ranged tanks.

- Spawner — spawns Minions for protection; low personal stats but dangerous if unaddressed.

- Assassin — lurks at the edge of the battlefield for high burst damage; low survivability.

- Sniper — slow, squishy Ranged unit with long range and an Aim requirement before firing.

- Commando — highly mobile Melee and/or Ranged, varying damage.

- Kamikaze — self-destructs for large AoE damage.

- Shield-Bearer — high AC, varying HP, low Melee damage; may buff nearby allies.

- Healer — little to no damage; heals and/or buffs the lowest-HP, strongest, or nearest ally.

- Fighter — evenly balanced Melee unit built for sustained combat. Enemy Fighters often carry longer Combos.

- Transport — protects and delivers units to the front line. Only Units that occupy 1 tile may enter a Transport.

- Gunfighter — Medium-Range Ranged Infantry built for sustained gunfights; weak Melee unless upgraded.

- Door Kicker — short range, high burst potential, decent Melee.

- Veteran Tactician — grants battlefield-wide, AoE, single-target, or self buffs.

- Disabler — low initial damage but strong control: stuns, roots, pulls, debuffs, or taunts.

- Berserker — low AC, high Melee damage, strong self-buffs (Enrage, Regeneration).

- Devourer — Melee unit that gains buffs/heals on a killing blow.

- Tinkerer — uses static Deployable Gadgets (Regeneration Aura, Sentry Turret, Smoke Grenade, Anti-Ballistic Field) and buffs Bot/Mechanized allies.

- Light Infantry — lightly armored/armed; Melee or Ranged.

- Heavy Infantry — heavy Melee/AC, or heavy Ranged pierce with light AC.

- Drone — flying, usually Ranged; low survivability but agile. Most small Drones have the Evasive X trait, which adds X to the To-Hit requirement for any single-target Ranged attack against that Unit.


- O.B.O.L™ Bot (Organic-to-Bionic Ontological Link) — a humanoid robot operated by a "Ripped" human consciousness. High AC, varying HP, strong Melee, accurate at range. Takes 1–3 Unit Slots and is expensive to deploy.

- Battle Buddy™ Bot — a combat robot (treads, insectoid legs, wheels, etc.) built for destruction. High AC, varying HP, strong Melee, accurate at range, and can mount very heavy weapons. Takes 1–5 Unit Slots and is very expensive to deploy.

*Example — District 3 Hero: Archimedes, Augmented Metallurgist (Tinkerer archetype). Full stat blocks are maintained in the District 3 card file.*

# 4. Friendly Units, Troops & Unit Slots

## Terminology

Friendly Unit is the umbrella term for anything the Player controls on the Battlefield. It covers both of the following:

- **Hero — **a named, uniquely statted Friendly Unit. Each Strike Team has exactly 1. Heroes carry Action Slots, Leadership, and their Strike Team's Deck, and always begin on the Battlefield. See Section 3.

- **Troop — **a Friendly Unit deployed as part of a Strike Team that is not a Hero. Troops occupy Unit Slots and are summoned via Troop Cards.

Where a rule says "Friendly Unit," it applies to both. Where it says "Hero" or "Troop," it applies only to that specific type.

## Troop Stats

| **Stat** | **Abbrev.** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Health Points | HP | Damage a Troop can take before being Incapacitated. |
| Armor Class | AC | Reduces the likelihood an attack deals damage. |
| Integrity | INT | Armour against Cyber. Starts equal to the Troop's Armor Class. | 1–20 |
| Movement Speed | MS | Tiles the Unit can move in a single Combat turn. |
| Attack Range | — | Printed on each Ranged Action rather than on the Unit, so one Unit may carry Actions of different reach. |
| Unit Slots | — | How many Unit Slots the Troop occupies on the Strike Team Roster. |

As with Heroes, Damage is not a Troop stat — it is carried by the individual Actions a Troop performs.

Every Troop also carries a Combat Role, which governs the Action, Command, and Equipment Cards it may use. See Section 2.

## Unit Slots

Each Troop occupies a number of Unit Slots depending on its power. The Unit Slots available at the start of a combat equal the combined Leadership of the Heroes entering it, at a 1:1 ratio — a Hero with Leadership 6 may begin with Troops occupying up to 6 slots.

Unit Slots govern only the Troops that **start** a combat: those chosen from the Deck before it begins, up to the Leadership of the Heroes present. Some situations favor a small number of tough, slot-heavy Units; others favor many cheap ones.

Troops played from the Deck during combat are **not capped.** Each one costs its Aether, and must be placed on an open Player Spawn Tile. If none is free, a Troop Card cannot be played — keeping your Spawn Tiles clear is part of the fight.

## Losing & Recovering Units

When a Troop is Incapacitated, its Card is placed in the Deck's Discard Pile; until then the Card stays with the Troop on the Battlefield, and an Equipment Card stays with the Unit wearing it until it is removed or that Unit is Incapacitated. During Combat, a Troop can be returned to the Player's hand with a Repair Kit, then replayed at full Aether cost. At the end of Combat every discarded Card returns to the Deck regardless, so no Troop is ever permanently lost. See Section 6.

Troop Subtypes have been folded into the Archetype list in Section 3, which applies equally to Hero and Troop design.

# 5. Enemies, Bosses & Mini-Bosses

Behind every corner and down every dark hall lurks incomprehensible monstrosities. In this new, dark KETER, only the strong survive.

An Enemy is a hostile NPC encountered in Combat. Each has Health Points, Armor Class, Movement Speed, Integrity, Affinity, Skull Level, behaviour keywords and a Combo of Actions.

## Enemy Stats

| **Stat** | **Abbrev.** | **Description** | **Typical Range** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Health Points | HP | Total damage an Enemy can take before being Incapacitated. | Min 10 / Max 50 |
| Armor Class | AC | Reduces the likelihood an attack deals damage. | Min 3 / Max 18 |
| Movement Speed | MS | Tiles an Enemy can move in one Combat turn. | 0–6 |
| Integrity | INT | Armour against Cyber. Starts equal to the Enemy's Armor Class. | 1–20 |

Every Enemy carries a Combat Role as well. Enemies do not equip cards, so for them the Role exists purely so that Player card effects can target them by it — for example, an Action that strikes all Melee Units on the Battlefield.

## Affinity & Skulls

Each Enemy has an Affinity to 1 of the 6 District fields of research, shown by symbol and color on its card. Enemies typically only appear in their correlating District. An Enemy's Skull Level is displayed on its card — the higher the Skulls, the tougher the Enemy (scaled 1–6 for non-Boss/Mini-Boss Enemies). Skulls also represent bounty value when turned in at the Hangar.

## How Enemies Act

Revision 15.5: Intelligence and the Combat Logic Cards are retired. An Enemy's behaviour keywords decide how it acts.

## Action Selection (Combos)

Enemy Cards can have Combos used in strict sequential order — if Enemy A used Action 1 last turn, it uses Action 2 next. If the Combo is ever interrupted (the Enemy fails to use an Action), it restarts at Action 1. Battlefield control is therefore critical to keep Enemies from reaching their maximum damage output. Skull Level and Action count don't have to correlate.

Some Enemies also have Special Abilities — Persistent or Condition-Dependent effects outside their Combo — which override the next Combo Action and can be used to tune difficulty.

Revision 15.6: an Enemy may also carry **Passives and Auras**. They are not part of its Combo and are never "used"; they work exactly as a Troop's do (see Passives, Section 9).

## Bosses & Mini-Bosses

Bosses are built the same way as weaker Enemies but far more challenging, usually with dedicated Bodyguards that spawn alongside them (and may periodically summon more). They drop unique loot on defeat — specific Combat Cards, Troop and Hero Cards, Chips, and/or Prototypes.

Mini-Bosses are not kept in the Enemy Card Piles. They arrive through Dimensional Instability, below. They also drop unique loot on defeat.

Mini-Bosses and Bosses use the same stat line as Enemies — HP, AC, MS, INT — but are not bound by the Enemy ranges. Mini-Boss HP typically runs to 90 and Boss HP to 200, with AC and Integrity at or near their ceilings. A Boss carries the Skull Level of its POI.

## Elites

An Elite sits between an ordinary Enemy and a Mini-Boss: tougher and more dangerous than the Enemies around it, well short of a Mini-Boss. Elites are their own card type, using the Enemy stat line. As a guideline their HP runs from about 30 to 70, with Armor Class and Integrity toward the upper end of the Enemy ranges.

Like Mini-Bosses, Elites are not kept in the Enemy Card Piles. They arrive only through Dimensional Instability.

## Dimensional Instability

Every District the Player clears destabilises the rest of KETER. For each District cleared, every Enemy spawn carries a **3% chance** of producing something worse: 3% after one District, 6% after two, up to 15% after five.

Whenever an Enemy Spawn Tile draws its Enemy, roll a d100. At or under the current chance, an Elite or a Mini-Boss appears in place of the drawn Enemy: roll a d6, and on 1–4 it is an Elite, on 5–6 a Mini-Boss — two times in three an Elite, one time in three a Mini-Boss. It is chosen at random from those of the current District, and takes the tile regardless of the tile's Skull Level. The Enemy that was drawn goes to the bottom of its pile. If the current District has no Elite (or no Mini-Boss, whichever was rolled), nothing unusual comes: the Enemy that was drawn arrives after all (Revision 15.8).

Instability reaches only fights at **Skull Level 4 and above**, the Skull Level of the POI or ambush being fought. A fight at Skull 1 to 3 has none, however many Districts are cleared (Revision 16.5).

With no Districts cleared the chance is 0%, so a fresh Player meets no Elites or Mini-Bosses outside a Boss's own guard.

## Enemy Archetypes

**Archetypes shared with Heroes and Troops** — Juggernaut, Spawner, Assassin, Sniper, Commando, Kamikaze, Shield-Bearer, Healer, Fighter, Transport, Gunfighter, Door Kicker, Disabler, Berserker, Devourer — are defined once in §Hero & Troop Archetypes. An Enemy carrying one behaves as described there, targeting its own side's allies. Only the Archetypes below are Enemy-only.







- Swarm — weak alone, stronger the more are adjacent; may unlock a Special Attack at a threshold count.









- General — tactically gifted unit that directs/buffs allies.



- Necromancer — re-summons incapacitated allies at partial HP (as themselves or a weak generic "Shambler").


- Abomination — attacks all units; often paired with a second archetype.

- Chaff — weak, dumb, average speed; dangerous only in numbers.

# 6. Combat

## Starting Condition

All Units — Friendly and Enemy alike — enter the Battlefield at full HP at the start of every Combat. The only exception is when an active combat effect states otherwise. Damage taken in one Combat does not carry into the next.

Nothing carries between Combats. Damage does not persist, and every Combat Card discarded during a Combat — including Incapacitated Troops and the Troops that started the Combat — returns to the Deck once that Combat ends. Incapacitation is a cost paid within a single Combat, not a lasting one.

## Decks

A Strike Team has **one Deck** of 10 to 60 Combat Cards, shared by all of its Heroes. Troop, Equipment, Command and Aether Cards go in it; Action Cards never do (Section 3). A card of any Affinity may be included — but see Section 2 for why the matching Aether Chips matter.

Before the combat begins, any Starting Troops are taken out of the Deck (Section 3). At the start of combat, 6 cards are drawn. During each Draw Phase thereafter, 1 card is drawn per Hero currently on the Battlefield. If the Deck is empty, the cards in the discard pile, all but Troop cards, are shuffled to form a new Deck (Revision 16.4); if there are none, the Draw Phase is skipped.

## Turn Sequence

- Waves — if the current wave has been cleared or the wave timer has run out, the next wave arrives. See Waves, below.
- Aether Generation — each Hero on the Battlefield generates Aether of its own Affinity equal to its Affinity Level. A Hero with Affinity Level 4 in District 3 generates 4 District 3 Aether every turn. A Hero with two Affinities generates for both.
- Aether Draw — the Player randomly selects Aether Chips from the Aether Pouch equal to their **Player Level**, and places them in the Aether Bank on the Combat Datapad. A Level 1 Player draws 1 Chip, a Level 4 Player draws 4. This is in addition to what the Heroes generate. Drawn Chips stay in the Bank for the rest of the combat, so the Bank grows every turn until the Pouch is empty.

- Draw Phase — draw 1 card per Hero currently on the Battlefield (6 at the start of combat).

- Recharge Aether — during the Recharge Phase, all Spent Aether Chips are flipped face-up again to allow further Aether generation. **The round begins here** (Revision 15.6): the Statuses that arrived in the Player's half of the last round tick (Revision 15.9; see Statuses, Section 9), always-on Passives do their one-off effects, and anything waiting on *when the round begins* happens.

- Play cards / use abilities / use consumables (optional).

- Move characters.

- Play cards / use abilities / use consumables (optional).

- Characters attack (Action Phase).

- Play cards / use abilities / use consumables (optional).

- The Player's turn ends — the Statuses that arrived in the Enemies' half of the last round tick (Revision 15.9).

- Enemies move.

- Enemies use Actions.

If a Friendly or Enemy Unit's HP is reduced to 0, its Minifigure is removed from the Battlefield and its Card is placed in the respective Discard Pile. The Strike Team's Deck has one Discard Pile; all Enemies share a single Discard Pile.

Unspent Aether in the Aether Bank is lost at the end of the turn unless an active card states otherwise. This is distinct from NRG, which does carry over.

## Line of Sight

A Unit can see a target if **any** point in the tile it stands on can be joined
to **any** point in the target's tile by a straight line that touches no **wall**.
If a single such line exists, the shot is on; a wall has to shut out every line
between the two tiles to block it. A line that touches a wall at all \u2014 even
its very end \u2014 does not count, so two walls meeting at a corner seal it. Nothing else blocks sight: Full and Half Cover make a target
harder to hit without hiding it, and Units never block a shot at someone behind
them.

An Action may only be used on a target **within its range and in line of
sight**.

## Targeting

A Target may be a Unit or a **tile**. An area effect is centred on whichever it
names, so *all units within 2 of target tile* is aimed at ground and catches
whoever is standing near it, friend or foe.

**Target ally** means another friendly Unit, never the Unit itself; an effect
that affects its own Unit says **this Unit**.

A Target may name a subtype, and may name more than one: *target friendly
Ranged or Cyber Unit* is legal, and either subtype satisfies it.

## Reactions

A Unit may **hold fire** instead of attacking, using **Suppressive Fire**. It
pays the Action's Energy at the moment it holds, and then, during the Enemy
turn, it shoots the first Enemy that moves within range and line of sight. The
reaction shot itself costs nothing further.

Only a Unit whose card grants **Suppressive Fire** may hold, and only if it has
not moved that turn. The Enemy it shoots takes the damage and carries on moving.
An unused hold lapses at the end of the Enemy turn: the Energy is spent either
way.

A Unit that is **Pinned** is always hit by such a shot.

## Waves

Enemies do not arrive all at once. Every Floor is fought in **6 waves**, so a Strike Team has time to bring its Troops in before it is surrounded.

- **Each wave fills every Enemy Spawn Tile** on the Floor. Each tile draws an Enemy of its own Skull Level from the Enemy Card Pile, using the Reveal Rule: reveal cards until one of that Level appears, and place the rest at the bottom.

- **Every draw is subject to Dimensional Instability** (Section 5). An Elite or Mini-Boss that appears takes the tile whatever its Skull Level.

- **The next wave arrives** when the current wave has been cleared, or when the **wave timer** runs out — whichever comes first. The wave timer is a number of turns, chosen by the Player before the combat.

- **The pile is finite.** When no card of a tile's Level remains, that tile stays empty. When the pile is empty, no more Enemies arrive.

The Combat is **won** when every Enemy is down and nothing more can arrive — after the sixth wave, or sooner if the Enemy Card Pile runs out. It is **lost** only when every Hero present is Incapacitated; see Section 7.

## Incapacitation & Recovery

A Unit reduced to 0 HP is Incapacitated. Its Minifigure leaves the Battlefield, and a Troop's Card goes to the Deck's Discard Pile.

A Troop can be brought back mid-Combat with the appropriate Consumable. A Repair Kit returns a Troop's Troop Card from the Discard Pile to the hand.

Heroes cannot be recovered mid-Combat. When a Hero is Incapacitated, that Hero and the Action Cards assigned to them leave the Battlefield for the remainder of the Combat. The Deck, hand, Discard Pile and every Troop stay in play, and the Heroes still standing fight on — even if the fallen Hero was the leader. The Combat is lost only when every Hero present is Incapacitated.

Because a Hero's death removes their entire force, Heroes are the highest-value targets on the Battlefield for both sides. Protecting them is a core tactical concern; Enemy Combat Logic Levels 5 and 6 target them deliberately.

At the end of Combat, every discarded Card returns to the Deck and all Troops are available again at full HP. Heroes are the exception: a Hero Incapacitated during a deployment stays out, along with the Action Cards assigned to them, until the Strike Team returns to the Hangar. See Section 7 for deployment rules and the retry system.

## Token Cards

A Token is a subtype rather than a card type: a card may be a Troop Token or an
Equipment Token. Tokens are never drawn and never placed in a Deck — they are
summoned by another Card, Action or Effect. A Token lasts until the end of
Combat, and once it leaves the Battlefield it ceases to exist rather than
returning anywhere. Troop Tokens do not occupy Unit Slots. Tokens are never paid
for, so a Token Card prints the word Token where its Aether cost would sit.

## Energy

Energy is the resource a Unit spends to act. It was called Action Points in
revisions up to 13.3; the abbreviation **AP now means Armor Penetration**, and
Energy is abbreviated **NRG**. Heroes generate Energy each turn and carry unspent
Energy over. Troops always hold 3 and do not carry over.

## Troop Energy

Energy is a Troop stat with a range of **3 to 6**, set per card. Troop NRG
resets at the start of each turn and does not carry over. Moving costs 1 NRG
regardless of how many tiles are covered, up to the Troop's Movement Speed.
Troop Actions cost between 0 and 6 NRG, printed on the Action. A Troop may take
as many Actions in a turn as it can pay for, provided any other conditions on
those Actions are met.

On a Troop card, Energy is drawn as a segmented bar between the art window and
the stat bar, with one section per point of Energy. An Action's cost is shown as
that many filled sections of the same bar rather than as a numeral, so the cost
and the budget are read in the same units.

## Attack Types

**Ranged Attacks: **Must both Penetrate Armor Class and hit their target. Accuracy is rolled with a d20 against the To-Hit value printed on the Action being used. Cover and the Evasive trait add to that value; nothing else modifies it. Each Ranged Action prints a single Attack Range in tiles, and a target beyond that range cannot be attacked with it.

**Melee Attacks: **Always hit, but must still Penetrate Armor Class to deal damage.

**Aether Attacks: **Always hit and always Penetrate Armor Class, but require Aether — either played as an Aether Card or spent on an activated Ability. Damage caused by an Aether Card is considered Aether Damage and is not affected by Armor Class.

**Support Actions: **Always hit, since they target friendly Units. A Support Action whose only target is *this Unit* needs no target chosen: it is used on the Unit itself, at once, for its Energy (Revision 16.2).

## Armor Class & Penetration

A d20 is used to determine whether an attack Penetrates Armor Class. The defender's Armor Class (AC) is printed on their card; the attacker's roll must be equal to or greater than that score to Penetrate. Penetration bonuses (e.g. "Armor Penetration 2") are added directly to the attacker's roll.

An attack must both hit and Penetrate Armor Class in order to deal any damage. **What it carries needs the same** (Revision 16.0): the Statuses, Pushes, extra damage and every other effect written on a Melee, Ranged or Cyber attack land only when it Penetrates (a Cyber attack Penetrates Integrity instead). A blow that hits but does not get through does nothing at all. Support Actions and Aether Damage are not affected by Armor Class, so what they carry always lands.

## Cover

Cover does not grant Armor Class. It applies a To-Hit penalty to attackers firing from the opposite side of the barricade:

- **Half Cover Barricade (HCB) — **A barricade tile. The +2 To-Hit rule for it is in §Cover.

- **Full Cover Barricade (FCB) — **A barricade tile. The +4 To-Hit rule for it is in §Cover.

- Units larger than 1 hex tile do not receive Cover bonuses.

## Cyber Actions & Integrity

**Cyber** is a subtype alongside Melee, Ranged and Support, and Actions carry it the same way. A Unit needs the Cyber subtype to have a Cyber Action.

A Cyber Action **reaches and aims like a Ranged attack** — Attack Range, line of sight, the roll to hit — and then must **Penetrate Integrity rather than Armor Class**. Armor Penetration (AP) adds to that roll exactly as it does against Armor. A Cyber Action applied to a friendly Unit is not resisted.

**Integrity (INT)** is carried by every Unit, from 1 to 20. Revision 15.5 replaced Will and Security with it, and removed Overwhelmed and Compromised. A card with no Integrity uses its Armor Class.

## Archetypes

An Archetype is a **targeting label and nothing more**. It gives a card no
ability of its own; it exists so other cards can name it — "target a Sniper",
"all friendly Berserkers". A card may carry up to three.

Archetypes are independent of subtypes. Where an Archetype's description below suggests a way of
playing, that is guidance for building the card, not a rule the card enforces.

New Archetypes may be added in the Card Forge. A custom Archetype needs only a
name, since the name is the whole of what other cards target.

Revision 15.6: **every Archetype is a narrowing word** in the keyword library,
custom ones included, the moment they exist — *target Sniper ally*, *when a
Berserker enemy is Incapacitated*. The origins retired in Revision 15.5
(Human, Cyborg, Mechanical) are not.

## Combat Keywords

A word printed in *italics* inside an effect is a **combat keyword**: a defined
term whose meaning is written once and referred to thereafter. *Rooted* and
*Marked* are among those the rules define; the rest are yours to write.

A keyword changes nothing by itself. The effect that names it does the work —
"apply *Rooted*" is the instruction, and the keyword says what
*Rooted* then means.

## Variable Costs

An Aether cost may be printed as **X**, **XX** or **XXX** instead of a number.
X is chosen by the Player as the card is played, and the card's text says what X
does. XX and XXX mean the same choice made twice or three times over, each
paid separately: at X = 2, a cost of XX is 4 Aether.

Revision 15.8 settles how:

- **X is at least 1**, and at most what the Bank can pay. A card that costs X cannot be played for nothing.
- **X stands wherever a keyword takes a number.** *damage X*, *heal X*, *+X HP*, *Slowed X*, *for X turns*, *Deploy X Aether Battery*. Once the card is played its text reads with the number chosen. X written in plain words, outside a keyword, is only words.
- **A stat may be written N(X)**, N for each X. A Troop with Health 5(X) played for X = 3 arrives with 15 Health. Its own Actions and Passives read with that X for as long as it stands.
- **Unpaid, X is 1.** A variable-cost Troop that arrives without being paid for (a Starting Troop, or one put down by another card's Deploy) has X = 1. Recovered and played again from the hand, it is paid for and X is chosen as usual.
- **An Action Card with a variable Aether cost** asks for X each time it is used.
- **X needs a variable cost.** X in the text or stats of a card whose Aether cost has no X stands for nothing, and the Card Forge flags it.

A variable cost counts as its own entry on an Aether curve rather than as a
number, because its real cost is not known until it is played. For the Aether
Pouch's minimum it counts as the least it can be: 1 for X, 2 for XX, 3 for XXX.

## World Map Tiles

A **World Map Tile** is both a card and a piece. The hexagon printed in its art
window is the tile you cut out and place; the card around it carries the rules
that tile brings into play.

The hexagon shows the tile's **District** as its colour, a **name band**, and
optionally a **symbol** drawn from the same library the POI Sandbox uses. Tiles
are pointy-top and tessellate with each other.

A Tile is not a Combat Card. It is placed while building the world map, not
played from a hand, and it has no Aether cost.

## Aether Generation

Aether arrives from two places each turn, and both happen before anything is
played.

**Heroes generate directly.** A Hero on the Battlefield produces Aether of its
own Affinity equal to its **Affinity Level** — Level 4 in District 3 means 4
District 3 Aether, every turn, for as long as it is standing. A Hero with two
Affinities generates for each of them. This needs no Chip and cannot be Spent
away; it is simply income.

**Chips are drawn on top of that.** The Player draws Aether Chips from the
Aether Pouch equal to their **Player Level** — one at Level 1, four at Level 4 —
and places them in the Aether Bank. Each Chip generates the Aether value printed
on it. Drawn Chips stay in the Bank for the rest of the combat: they are marked
Spent when used and turned face-up again in the Recharge Phase, so the Bank grows
every turn until the Pouch runs out.

The two together are what a turn has to spend. Generation scales with the
Heroes you field; draws scale with how far you have progressed. District Aether
may also pay the generic part of any cost.

## The Aether Pouch

Before each combat the Player fills the Strike Team's **one Aether Pouch** from
the Aether Chips they own. It is the only Pouch, and it feeds the only Aether
Bank, whatever the number of Heroes.

**Minimum.** The Pouch must hold at least as many Chips as the full Aether cost —
generic plus District — of the most expensive card in the Deck. A Deck whose
priciest card costs 6 needs at least 6 Chips. This counts Chips, not the Aether
they are worth.

**No maximum.** Because drawn Chips stay in the Bank, a larger Pouch simply lets
the Bank keep growing for longer.

**District matching.** A Deck holding cards that need a District's Aether, with no
Chip of that District in the Pouch, is legal — but those cards cannot be paid for.
Check before you go.

**Basic Aether Chips.** Six Basic Chips, one for each District, are always
available in any quantity. Each generates 1 Aether of its District and has no
effect.

**Chip value.** Every Chip prints its own Aether value, from 0 to 10. Section 12
gives the guideline for choosing it — Level sets a base and Rarity adds a bonus —
but the printed value is what counts.

**Effect Chips.** Spending a Chip that carries an effect generates its Aether
and fires its effect as well. A Chip with no District generates Generic Aether,
which pays any cost.

## Healing and Gaining Health

**Healing restores what was lost.** A Unit healed above its printed Health
Points is simply returned to that figure; the surplus is wasted.

**Gaining Health is a different thing.** Where an effect says a Unit *gains*
Health rather than being healed, it may go above its printed figure and stays
there until the surplus is lost. Damage comes off the surplus first.

The distinction matters because the two appear on different cards: a Repair Kit
or Medical Supplies heals, while a Consumable or a buff that grants Health adds
to it.

## Enemy Behavior

An Enemy card may carry **Behavior keywords**, printed at the foot of its rules
box. They decide what the Enemy does when you run it, so that no judgement call
falls to the player.

**The default, when a card carries none:** attack the closest Unit. Ties go to
the lowest current Health, then the lowest Armor Class, then the lowest card
number. That last tie-break exists so an Enemy's target is never a matter of
opinion.

**Targeting keywords** choose who it attacks:

| Keyword | It attacks |
| --- | --- |
| **Cunning** | the nearest Hero, ignoring Troops |
| **Sadistic** | the Unit with the lowest current Health |
| **Opportunistic** | the Unit with the lowest Armor Class |
| **Brutish** | the Unit with the highest Armor Class |
| **Vindictive** | the last Unit that attacked it, hit or miss |
| **Ravenous** | a Unit another Enemy has already attacked this round |
| **Methodical** | a Unit carrying no Enemy-applied status |

**Movement keywords** choose where it goes:

| Keyword | It does |
| --- | --- |
| **Cautious X** | moves away from any Player Unit within X tiles, then acts normally |
| **Cowardly X** | at X Health or below, moves away from the closest Player Unit and takes no Action |

**Player Units may carry them too**, up to three each. Seven mirror an Enemy
keyword so a fight reads the same from either side of the table, and three have
no Enemy counterpart because your side has roles the Enemy side does not.

| Keyword | It attacks |
| --- | --- |
| **Valiant** | the nearest Boss or Mini-Boss, ignoring lesser Enemies |
| **Decisive** | the Enemy with the lowest current Health |
| **Precise** | the Enemy with the lowest Armor Class |
| **Dauntless** | the Enemy with the highest Armor Class |
| **Defiant** | the last Enemy that attacked it |
| **Coordinated** | an Enemy an ally has already attacked this round |
| **Thorough** | an Enemy carrying no status yet |

| Keyword | It does |
| --- | --- |
| **Wary X** | moves away from any Enemy within X tiles, then acts normally |
| **Prudent X** | at X Health or below, withdraws and takes no Action |
| **Steadfast** | holds its tile and does not move |
| **Medic** | uses a Support Action on the most wounded ally before attacking |
| **Guardian** | stays within 1 tile of your Hero rather than chasing targets |

**Keywords stack, and a conditional one outranks an unconditional one.** An
Enemy that is *Cautious 2, Cowardly 5* kites while healthy and flees once it
drops to 5 Health, because Cowardly only applies in a situation and Cautious
always does. Where two conditions are both met, the keyword printed first wins.

Two keywords that both always apply — *Cunning* and *Brutish*, say — leave the
second doing nothing. The Card Forge flags that rather than letting it pass.

## Equipment Modifiers

An Equipment Card's stat bar holds **changes, not values**. A rifle granting +5
Health Points shows +5 HP; it does not itself have 5 Health Points. Only the
stats a card actually changes appear on its bar, and a gain prints with its
plus sign so a bonus and a penalty are never confused.

Equipment effects may carry an Energy cost, paid by the Unit using them.

## Auras

**Aura** is a kind of Passive. It carries a **Radius** in tiles and affects only
Units within that radius of the Unit carrying it. Which Units it affects —
allies, enemies, or both — is stated in the Aura's own text.

An Aura is always on while its Unit is on the Battlefield, except where a card
or effect suppresses it. Like any always-on Passive (Revision 15.6) it holds for
as long as a Unit stays within reach, and ends the moment it steps out.

## Status Effects

Positive or negative Status Effects can be applied by friendly or hostile Units, cards, abilities, and Prototypes. Unless stated otherwise, Status Effects last only 1 turn. A Status given by an always-on Passive — a Prototype's among them — lasts for as long as the Passive does (Revision 15.6).

## Damage Types

Damage types are relevant only for determining Armor Class Penetration, with the exception of Aether Damage, which ignores Armor Class entirely.

# 7. Exploration & the World Map

Once a Roster is assembled, the Player navigates the World Map one tile at a time.

## World Map Movement Speed

World Map Movement Speed is calculated as the average Movement Speed of all Heroes being deployed — values of 0 are excluded, and the result is rounded up to the nearest whole number. Troops are not counted. Only 1 unexplored tile can be traveled to per turn, though additional already-cleared tiles may be crossed.

## Deployment Rules

A deployment brings **one Strike Team** of up to 3 Heroes, sharing one Deck and one Aether Pouch.

Floor capacity determines how many of those Heroes enter a given Combat — 1 for a Small Floor, 2 for Medium, 3 for Large. Before each Combat the Player chooses one Hero to **lead** it: the leader always enters, and other Heroes fill any remaining capacity. Heroes brought but not entering are held in reserve.

Leading has no other effect. If the leader is Incapacitated, the Heroes still standing fight on.

### Floor Size Restrictions by Player Level

POIs cannot present Floors larger than the Player can field:

- **Level 1 — **Small Floors only.

- **Level 2 — **Small or Medium Floors.

- **Level 3 and above — **any Floor size.

### Losing a Combat & Retrying

A Combat is lost only when **every Hero present** is Incapacitated. The Player may immediately re-attempt it provided at least 1 reserve Hero remains, choosing a new leader and bringing in reserve Heroes to replace those lost. Retrying costs nothing — the Deployment Inventory, Consumables, and Prototypes are all preserved. A Combat may be re-attempted any number of times while reserves last.

Because a Floor fields as many of the Strike Team's Heroes as it has room for, a Strike Team on a Floor large enough for all of its Heroes has no reserves and therefore no retry. Bringing more Heroes than a Floor admits is what buys a safety net.

### Deployment Wipe

If every deployed Hero is Incapacitated and no reserve Hero remains, the deployment ends in a wipe. The Player loses the entire Deployment Inventory, all Consumables, and all equipped Prototypes, and is returned to the Hangar.

Heroes, the Strike Team's Deck and its Action Card assignments are always preserved through a wipe. Nothing in the Hangar Inventory is ever at risk.

### Access Lock

Decks cannot be modified or changed at any point during a deployment — only at the Hangar. Loot collected along the way enters the Deployment Inventory but cannot be equipped, played, or added to a Deck until the Strike Team returns.

Consumables may be selected or swapped from the Deployment Inventory between POIs, but never while inside one. Whatever Consumables a Strike Team carries into a POI are what it has for the duration.

### Incapacitated Heroes During a Deployment

A Hero Incapacitated in Combat is unavailable for the remainder of the deployment, along with the Action Cards assigned to them, even if the Player goes on to clear the POI. The Deck is shared and stays with the Strike Team. They return only when the Strike Team reaches the Hangar. An Incapacitated Hero does not count as a reserve.

## Discovery Tiles

A Discovery Tile is any tile not previously explored. A small marker token is placed on every unexplored tile; when the Player enters it, the token is removed and a Discovery Card is drawn from its pile. Only 1 Discovery Tile can be revealed per turn.

- Random Event — minor Proficiency Checks that reward or punish the Player, an Ambush, a tense Survivor encounter, or a run-in with a traveling Scav Merchant.

- Ambush — draw 1 Ambush Card, which details the number of Levels to draw and the reward for completing the combat. Eliminate all Enemies in that standalone Combat to claim it.

- Point of Interest (POI) — a location explored as a series of Floors. Draw 1 POI Card of matching Player Level and assemble and clear the POI as instructed on the card. See Section 8.

## Investigation Cards

If the Player moves and does not land on an unexplored tile, they draw 1 Investigation Card before moving again. Investigation Cards include bits of lore, Affinity Checks with minor punishments/rewards, occasional Scav Merchant encounters, and Survivor encounters that may or may not be helpful. Lore Cards contain military reports, research notes, personal notes, warnings, and other world-building content.

## Scav Merchant

An NPC found as a random encounter. Functions as a mobile Shop that draws 4 Loot Cards of the Player's Level and 2 that are 1 Level higher.

# 8. Points of Interest (POI)

A Point of Interest is a location explored using the Battlefield Map. Some POIs are more dangerous than others, but rewards scale with the challenge. Prerequisites, when they exist, are stated directly on the POI card.

## POI Types

**Black Lab: **One per District, placed randomly. Contains Unique Bosses and Loot. Requires 1 Black Keycard per entry. Clearing it unlocks Hangar shop access to some of that District's loot and Units, and raises Player Level by 1.

**Lab: **Contains Prototypes, Healing Items, Keycards, and other rare items.

**Security Armory: **Holds Equipment, Armor, and Weapons from the District's absent Security Forces. Powerful loot, often heavily guarded. Requires the Security Keycard Prototype for entry.

**Bunker: **Contains advanced Weapons/Equipment/Armor, often District-themed. Very difficult; requires an Exec Keycard.

**Overrun Hideout: **Remains of a reinforced structure once inhabited by survivors. May offer Quests, Recon, or a recruitable Survivor Hero.

**Unique POI: **A fully pre-selected series of Floors, possibly with unique names, Special Objective Floors, Enemies, Bosses, and rewards — second in risk/reward only to a District Black Lab.

## Determining POI Difficulty

POI difficulty always matches Player Level. If a POI is an exception, that special condition is stated directly on the POI Card.

## Floor Construction

A Floor is a single Battlefield overlay. Each POI is built from pre-generated Floors printed on 8.5×11 inch hex-grid sheets. The number of sheets used is always 6 + [POI Level]. Floors are labeled and sorted by District, Type, and Level.

| **Floor Size** | **Sheets Required** | **Dimensions** | **Max Capacity** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | 1 sheet | 18 × 11 tiles | 1 Hero |
| Medium | 2 sheets (lettered) | 18 × 22 tiles | 2 Heroes |
| Large | 4 sheets (lettered) | 27 × 22 tiles | 3 Heroes |

Max Capacity limits how many of the Strike Team's Heroes enter a Combat on that Floor. It never reduces a Hero's Leadership: every Hero who enters adds theirs to the pool of Starting Troops.

Floor size is also limited by Player Level: Level 1 POIs use Small Floors only, Level 2 POIs use Small or Medium, and Level 3 and above may use any size. See Section 7.

Higher-Skull POIs feature Medium and Large Floors more frequently. Unless stated otherwise, every Floor's objective is to eliminate all Enemies, and each Floor's objective must be completed before proceeding to the next. Players begin battle by placing Units on any green-highlighted Spawn Tile.

## The Reveal Rule

When a rule calls for a specific type of Card — a Troop Card for recruitment, for instance — reveal Loot Cards of the matching Level until a Card of that type appears. Place all other cards revealed this way at the bottom of the Loot pool. Hero and Troop Cards are mixed into the general Loot pool for their Level and District rather than kept in separate piles, which keeps the number of card piles at the table low.

## Battlefield Tile Types

**Enemy Spawn Tile: **Draw an Enemy Card from the appropriate Enemy Card Pile and place matching Minifigures within the Spawn Tile. The District always matches the currently occupied District, and the Level is determined by the Skull Level printed on the Spawn Tile. Each Enemy Spawn Tile carries its own Skull Level, from 1 to 6. It is filled once per wave (Section 6), and every draw is subject to Dimensional Instability (Section 5).

**Loot Tile: **If a Friendly Unit lands here, draw a Loot Card from the pile matching the tile's District and Level; add it to the Deployment Inventory.

**Half Cover Barricade (HCB): **Units adjacent to an HCB gain +2 To-Hit against Ranged attacks from the opposite side.

**Full Cover Barricade (FCB): **Units adjacent to an FCB gain +4 To-Hit against Ranged attacks from the opposite side.

**Hive: **Spawns 1 Swarm-type monster of the matching District per turn until disabled. A Swarm Minifigure of the matching Level and biome is placed on the tile at the start of combat.

**Key Tile: **Rewards a key that unlocks a Locked Chest or Locked Door on that Floor. Consumed on use, and lost if not used.

**Locked Chest: **Rewards X Loot Cards of the Player's current Level, where X is the number printed on the tile.

**Locked Door: **Sometimes leads to an Emergency Exit, a Captive Survivor, a Healing Station, or additional loot.

**Captive Survivor: **When a Friendly Unit lands on this tile, apply the Reveal Rule to find a Unit or Hero Card. The Player controls the revealed Unit during the fight and may keep it if it survives to the completion of the Floor.

**Healing Station: **One-time-use per battle; heals all units within a 1-hex radius to full HP when a unit lands on it.

**Emergency Exit: **Lets the Player leave the POI early while keeping loot already found. The final reward is forfeited, and the POI is considered complete and cannot be re-entered — with the exception of Black Labs.

**Walls: **Shown as a shaded tile or a bold outline on the wall-bearing side(s) of a tile.

# 9. Loot, Currency & Consumables

## Loot

The party collects loot while exploring the Districts. Loot rarity, from most common to most powerful: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic. Loot includes Troop Cards, Consumables, Aether Cards, Action Cards, Hero Cards, Command Cards, and Equipment Cards.

**Cards in the haul** (Revision 16.2). An Elite, a Mini-Boss or a Boss, a Cache, and a POI's reward can each drop a Consumable or a Prototype card: one of that District's (or of no District), of a rarity the Skull Level allows (Skull 1 Common, Skull 2 up to Uncommon, and so on). It goes into the haul with the rest, and is kept at the dock once the haul is brought home. A Boss always drops one; a Mini-Boss half the time; an Elite a quarter of the time; a Cache a fifth; a POI's reward three times in ten. Chips stay free.

## Prototypes

The Player may equip any number of Prototypes, which provide simple, persistent buffs to the team while in combat. Prototype Status Effects are always active. All equipped Prototypes are lost in a Deployment Wipe — that is, when every deployed Hero is Incapacitated and no reserve Hero remains. See Section 7.

Keycards are a category of Prototype. Some Keycards are consumed on use and some are not — this is stated on the Prototype Card. Keycards are never Consumables.

## Effect Text

A card's effect text is written in plain English, and the words that carry rules
meaning are **coloured**. Everything in white is flavour: it reads on the table
and is ignored by the rules.

Seven colours, each a category (Revision 15.4):

| Colour | Category | Means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Violet | Condition | when the effect applies at all | *when this Unit takes damage*, *if the target has Slowed* |
| Cyan | Target | who or what it happens to | *target Sniper ally*, *all units within 2 tiles* |
| Amber | Effect | what happens | *damage 5*, *heal 3*, *Push 1* |
| Crimson | Status | a named state applied to a Unit | *Rooted*, *Poisoned 3* |
| Pink | Duration | how long | *for 3 turns*, *persistent* |
| Green | Modifier | changes how an Action works | *Burst 3*, *AoE 1*, *Pierce* |
| Blue | Stats | raises or lowers a stat | *+10 HP*, *−2 AC* |

**One sentence is one effect**, and its coloured words are read in this order:

> **Condition → Target → Effect → Modifier → Duration**

A card that does two things uses two sentences. So *"Target enemy takes 5
damage. All units within 2 take 3 damage."* is two effects, and there is never
any question which number belongs to which.

**A number travels with its keyword,** and takes its colour. *Rooted 3* is one
thing, not a keyword and a loose number, so the two can never come apart. There
is no separate colour for numbers, because there are no loose numbers: a stat
change is written with a **Stats** keyword, which carries its amount — *+2 AC*.

**Duration** is a number of turns, **persistent**, which lasts until something
removes it, or **while attached**, which lasts as long as the card granting it
stays on the Unit \u2014 so a bonus from a piece of Equipment ends when that
Equipment is removed. Left out, an Effect resolves at once, and **a Status
lasts 1 turn**.

A **Stats** keyword always needs a Duration, because a stat change that
resolves at once changes nothing.

Only words in the keyword library may be coloured, so anything marked is
something the rules \u2014 and the Battle Simulator \u2014 can carry out.

**How it prints** (Revision 15.6). Every distance says *tiles* — *within 2
tiles*, *Push 1 tile*. A Duration of turns reads *for 2 turns*. A narrowing word
prints inside the Target it narrows: written *all allies within 2* then
*District 3*, it reads *all District 3 allies within 2 tiles*. A sentence that
opens on a keyword opens with a capital.

**Building an effect.** In the Card Forge and the Chassis editor, the keyword
builder walks through one effect at a time — when it happens, who it is about,
who it affects, what it does, any Modifiers, how long it lasts — offering only
what that kind of ability may use, and ends on a review of the whole sentence.

## The Keyword Library

Each keyword says what its own number means, because they differ. On a card with a variable cost, any of those numbers may be written **X** (Revision 15.8; see Variable Costs, Section 6).

### Statuses

Revision 15.4: **every Status lasts its Duration at full strength, then ends.**
Its number, where it has one, is its strength \u2014 *Poisoned 3 for 2 turns*
deals 3 each of the two times it ticks. Statuses with no strength are written without
a number: *Rooted for 2 turns*.

| Keyword | Its number | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Rooted** | — | Cannot move. A lock (see below). |
| **Stunned** | — | Cannot act or move at all. A lock (see below). |
| **Slowed** | Movement lost | Movement Speed reduced by that much. |
| **Marked** | — | Ranged attacks against it always hit. A lock (see below). |
| **Pinned** | — | Suppressive Fire reaction shots against it always hit. A lock (see below). |
| **Poisoned** | damage each turn | Deals that much damage each time it ticks. |
| **Bleed** | — | Deals 10% of the Unit's remaining Health each time it ticks, rounded, never less than 1. |
| **Weakened** | damage lost | Deals that much less damage. |
| **Sundered** | Armor lost | Armor Class reduced by that much. |
| **Drained** | Energy lost | Generates that much less Energy at each refill. |
| **Evasive** | To-Hit added | Adds that much to the To-Hit threshold of Ranged Attacks against it. |
| **Hardened** | damage stopped | Each hit on it deals that much less, never below 1 (Revision 16.3). An always-on Passive keeps it on: *this Unit gains Hardened 1*. |

**Statuses apply outright.** A card that inflicts one inflicts it; there is no
roll to resist. **With no Duration, a Status lasts 1 turn.**

**A second dose adds both.** A Status landing on a Unit that already has it adds
its strength and its turns to what is there: *Poisoned 3 for 2 turns*, then
*Poisoned 1 for 3 turns*, makes *Poisoned 4 for 5 turns*. The locks are the
exception.

**The locks** (Revision 16.0). Stunned, Rooted, Pinned and Marked lock a Unit
down, so they follow a rule of their own, which keeps one from being held down
for a whole fight:

- **2 turns at most.** A longer Duration, *persistent* or *while attached* is
  cut to 2. The Card Forge flags them, and the keyword builder offers 1 or 2.
- **No second dose.** One landing on a Unit that already has it does nothing:
  it neither adds turns nor starts them again. A Burst gives one lock, not three.
- **A guard once it ends.** When a lock ends, the Unit cannot be given that one
  again until the same point of the next round. So a lock of 1 turn can take at
  most every other turn of a Unit's, and one of 2 turns two in three. A guarded
  Unit wears a hollow ring in the lock's colour beside its Status dots.
- **Never always on.** An always-on Passive (one with no *when*), and so an
  Aura, cannot give a lock. A Passive that reacts — *when the round begins*,
  *when this Unit is attacked* — can.

**A Status ticks at the end of the turn after the one it arrived in**
(Revision 15.9). When it ticks it applies its effect at full strength, then
loses a turn, and ends when it has none left.

The round has two halves. The Player's runs from the Recharge Phase to the end
of the Player's turn. The Enemies' is their turn and the Waves, Aether
Generation, Aether Draw and Draw phases that follow it.

- A Status that arrives in **the Player's half** ticks at the next Recharge
  Phase, once the Enemies have had their turn.
- A Status that arrives in **the Enemies' half** ticks as the Player's next
  turn ends, before the Enemies act.

So a Status of 1 turn always lasts through the whole of the next turn, whoever
gave it. An Enemy Stunned in the Player's turn loses its turn; a Hero Stunned
in the Enemies' turn loses theirs. A Unit that strengthens itself keeps that
through the other side's next turn, and has lost it by its own.

A second dose keeps the timing of the first. A Stats keyword with a Duration
in turns counts down the same way. Until Revision 15.9 every Status ticked in
the Recharge Phase, so one given in the Enemies' turn lost a turn before the
Player had taken one.

An Enemy Dashes at once, away from the Unit it attacked (or from the nearest of yours), as far as it can: hit and run. Given by an always-on Passive, one of yours has the tiles every turn, and an Enemy Dashes away once it has made its attacks. |

### Modifiers

Revision 15.4. A Modifier changes how an Action works rather than doing a thing
of its own.

| Keyword | Its number | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Burst** | attacks in all | The Action is made that many times, for **one** payment of its Energy. Each attack rolls to hit and to Penetrate, and applies the Action's effects, separately. The next target is chosen each time \u2014 a new one or the same \u2014 and when only one is in reach the Burst continues on it. A Burst runs to the end while a target remains in reach. An Enemy chooses each attack's target afresh, as it normally targets; a Unit holding fire with a Burst Action fires the whole Burst at the Enemy that triggered it. |
| **Pierce** | — | The attack carries on through to the Unit directly behind the target. |
| **Chain** | tiles | After it resolves, the attack jumps to the nearest other Enemy within that many tiles and deals the same damage. |
| **AoE** | tiles | Hits everything within that many tiles of the target, friend or foe, for full damage. |
| **Suppressive Fire** | — | See Reactions, Section 6. |
| **Overclock** | — | A Vantage Robotics mechanic still to be written. It has no rules in a fight yet, so it does nothing and costs nothing; the keyword builder no longer offers it, and the Card Forge warns where a card has it (Revision 16.0). |
| **Aura** | tiles | A Passive that reaches every Unit within that many tiles, for as long as they stay within it. |
| **Lunge** | tiles | On a Melee Action (Revision 16.1). Before it attacks, the Unit may move up to that many tiles to reach its target, so the target may be that many tiles further off. The move costs no Movement and no Energy, walks round walls and Units like any other, and must end beside the target with no wall between. Of the tiles that would do, it takes one with no hazard on it where there is one, then the shortest walk, then the one beside the most Cover, then the lowest on the map. The tile it lands on counts (a hazard bites), the move is an event (*moves*), and its tiles count toward Charge. A Burst lunges once, for its first attack; a Unit holding fire never lunges. |
| **Charge** | damage added | On a Melee Action (Revision 16.1). The attack deals that much more damage if the Unit has moved 3 tiles or more this turn before it lands. Walking, a Lunge, a Leap and a Dash all count; being Pushed does not. Its bonus shows in the attack's odds once it would apply. |
| **Line** | tiles | Revision 16.3. The attack also hits every Unit on the tiles running straight from the attacker through the target, that many tiles long, friend or foe, for the same damage. |
| **Cone** | tiles | Revision 16.3. The attack also hits every Unit in the wedge that widens from the attacker toward the target, that many tiles deep (within 30 degrees either side of the line to the target), friend or foe, for the same damage. |

Lunge and Charge **add** to what an attack is worth rather than multiply it: a
tile of reach, or a point of damage, each worth its keyword's weight.

### Targets and Narrowing

Revision 15.6. A Target says who an effect happens to; a **narrowing word**
written after it limits who counts.

| Keyword | Who |
| --- | --- |
| **target enemy** / **target ally** / **target tile** | The one chosen, within range and line of sight. In a Passive that reacts, the other Unit in the event — the attacker, or the one hit. A Unit's Action that says *target tile* is aimed at any tile within its range (1 if it has none) that the Unit can see, its own included, and what it says is carried out there with no attack roll (Revision 16.3, clarified in Companion 14.12). Auto never aims one. |
| **this Unit** | The Unit using the Action, or whose card it is. |
| **attached Unit** | The Unit this card is attached to (Equipment). |
| **that Unit** | Whoever the Condition is about: the one that took the damage, moved or was deployed. |
| **all enemies / allies / units within** | Everything of that side within that many tiles of the target — or, in a Passive, of this Unit. |
| **all enemies** / **all allies** | Everything of that side on the Battlefield, wherever it stands; *all allies* includes the one using it (Revision 16.3). For its power it counts like an area of 4 tiles (×3). |
| **adjacent** | Next to the target; on Deploy, beside the deploying Unit. |

| Narrowing word | Matches |
| --- | --- |
| **Melee**, **Ranged**, **Support**, **Cyber** | Units with that subtype. |
| **District** | Units of that District: *District 3*. |
| **Troop**, **Hero** | Troops, or Heroes (Revision 16.3). Every Troop is a mechanical robot, so a card that names Mechanical Units on your side says *Troop*: *all Troop allies*. |
| **any Archetype** | Units carrying it: *Sniper*, *Berserker*, and every custom Archetype. |
| **within** | In a Condition: Units that many tiles or fewer from this Unit. |

**Two of the same kind mean either** — *target Sniper or Gunfighter ally*.
**Different kinds mean both** — *target Melee Fighter enemy* is a Fighter that is
also Melee. A card aimed at a narrowed Target can only be aimed at a Unit that
matches.

**An area is worth more** (Revision 16.0). Where an effect's power is worked
out from its keywords, as the Workshop and the Card Forge do, a sentence aimed
at *all enemies*, *all allies* or *all units within* N tiles counts
(1 + 0.5 × N) times what it carries: ×1.5 within 1 tile, ×2 within 2, ×2.5
within 3. Reaching more Units is worth more.

| --- | --- |
| **HP** | Maximum Health, and current Health with it. When it ends, maximum Health returns and current Health is lowered to fit. |
| **AC** | Armor Class. |
| **MS** | Movement Speed. |
| **DMG** | The damage the Unit deals. |
| **INT** | Integrity, armour against Cyber. |

| **AP** | Armor Penetration. |

### Conditions

Revision 15.6. A Condition is **grammar**, built from three pieces:

> **connector → who it is about → an event or a state**

*when* + *this Unit* + *takes damage* reads **"When this Unit takes damage, …"**.

**Connectors**

| Keyword | What it does | Used in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **when** | Waits for an event. The effect happens **each time** the event does. | Passives |
| **if** | Checks something **as the effect resolves**; if it is not so, the effect does not happen. | Actions; in a Passive, only after a *when* |
| **while** | Keeps the effect on **for as long as** a state holds, and off the moment it does not. | Passives |

**Who it is about** is a Target word: *this Unit*, *attached Unit*, *the target*
(an Action's own target), *an ally* (any friendly Unit but this one), *an enemy*,
*any Unit*, or *that Unit* (whoever the *when* is about). *an ally*, *an enemy*
and *any Unit* take narrowing words: *when a Melee ally within 3 tiles is
Incapacitated*.

**Events** — what happens, for *when*:

| Keyword | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **takes damage** | Loses at least 1 Health to an attack or an effect. |
| **deals damage** | Its own attack or effect takes at least 1 Health from another Unit. Damage from a Status or a hazard does not count. |
| **kills an enemy** | Its own attack or effect Incapacitates an Enemy. Damage from a Status or a hazard does not count. |
| **is deployed** | Arrives on the Battlefield: at the start of a Combat, or when played or Deployed. |
| **is Incapacitated** | Is reduced to 0 Health or Sacrificed. |
| **attacks** / **is attacked** | Makes, or is the target of, an attack — whether or not it hits. |
| **moves** | Finishes a move of its own. Being pushed is not moving. |
| **ends its move within** | Finishes a move of its own that many tiles or fewer from this Unit. |
| **gains** | Is given the named Status: *gains Slowed*. |
| **the round begins** | The Recharge Phase of every round. It is about no one, so nothing comes before it. |

**States** — what is true, for *while* and *if*:

| Keyword | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **is below half HP** | Has less than half of its maximum Health. |
| **is at full HP** | Has all of its maximum Health. |
| **has** | Carries the named Status: *has Poisoned*. |
| **is within** / **is further than** | Stands that many tiles or fewer / more than that many tiles from this Unit. |

**In an Action**, *if* may also ask what the Action itself just did: *if the
target takes damage*, *if the target is Incapacitated*, *if this Unit deals
damage*, *if this Unit kills an enemy*.

**One trigger and one check at most.** An effect may carry one *when* and one
*if* — *"When this Unit takes damage, if this Unit is below half HP, this Unit
gains +2 AC for 2 turns."* — or a *while* on its own. Anything the Card Forge
cannot read as grammar is flagged.

**Conditions are not scored yet.** They add nothing to, and take nothing from,
an effect's power.

### Passives

Revision 15.6. A Passive is never used; it **applies whenever its card is
active**: a Unit's while it is on the Battlefield and not Incapacitated,
Equipment's while it is attached, a Prototype's for the whole Combat (acting
through the leading Hero), and an Action Card's while its Hero stands. Passives
are **exempt from subtypes**.

- **No Condition — always on.** Its Statuses and Stats simply hold, so a Stats
  keyword needs no Duration: *this Unit gains +1 INT* is +1 Integrity for as
  long as the card is active. What cannot simply be "on" — damage, healing, a
  Push, a Deploy, a remove — happens **once each round, at Recharge**.
- **while** — the same, for as long as its state holds, and off the moment it
  does not.
- **when** — it reacts: the effect happens each time its event does, and lasts
  as its Duration says (a Stats keyword needs one).

**A Passive never sets itself off.** One that reacts never fires from anything
its own effect started, however long the chain, so no pair of Passives can run
forever.

### Retired

**SPOOL** and **Splash** are withdrawn (Revision 16.3 rewrote every card that still named SPOOL, with Stats that build up over the rounds instead), and **Modify** became the Stats
keywords in Revision 15.4. So are two conditions that other
wording already covers: *if this penetrates* (use **if the target takes
damage**) and *when combat begins* (use **when this Unit is deployed**).

Revision 15.6 replaced the one-piece Conditions with grammar. A card still
using one is rewritten as it is read:

| Was | Now |
| --- | --- |
| *if damaged* | *if the target takes damage* |
| *when deployed* | *when this Unit is deployed* |
| *when Incapacitated* | *when this Unit is Incapacitated* |
| *if this Unit kills an Enemy* | *if this Unit kills an enemy* |
| *further than 3* | *if the target is further than 3 tiles* |
| *closer than 2* | *if the target is within 1 tile* |

## Combat Cards

Combat Cards make up a Deck, used during combat to shift the tide of battle. Cards cost Aether to play and can have passive or active effects. The subtypes are:

- Troop Cards — place a Minifigure on the Battlefield during combat.

- Equipment Cards — make Units and Heroes more effective by granting stat increases and new Actions. Any Unit may use Equipment of any Affinity; its Affinity sets only which Aether pays for it.

- Command Cards — cause a Troop to perform a unique Action, and are discarded after use. Any Troop may perform any Command.

- Aether Cards — harness the chaotic power of the Aether for destruction or creation. Damage from an Aether Card is Aether Damage and ignores Armor Class.

### Combat Role on Cards

Action, Command, and Equipment Cards each carry a Combat Role, shown the same way as on Troop Cards, describing the kind of effect they have — a Ranged shot, a Melee strike, a Support heal. Like Units, a card may carry any combination of Melee, Ranged, and Support.

Combat Role does not restrict who may use a card. Any Unit may use any card.

Aether Cards and Consumable Cards carry no Combat Role.

## Currencies

**Aether Shards: **The primary currency; used to buy cards, recruit Heroes/Units, and pay for various Survivor services. Also referred to simply as "Shards."

**Skulls: **Represent both an Enemy's difficulty and the bounty value earned by turning it in at the Hangar.

**Aether Chips: **Used to generate Aether Energy (also simply called "Aether"), which is then spent to play cards from the Player's Combat Deck. Chips also measure total Affinity with each District.

### Getting & Using Aether Chips

Chips are rare loot, most often found in Labs or Bunkers, with a small chance from Random Events. They can also be purchased from roaming traders or the Hangar Quartermaster Bot. The six Basic Chips, one per District, are always available and never need finding. Before each Combat the Player chooses which Chips to bring and places them in the Aether Pouch; each turn, Chips equal to the Player Level are drawn from it into the Aether Bank, where they stay for the rest of the Combat. Spending a Chip flips it face-down ("Spent") until it recharges the following turn. Unspent Aether is lost at the end of the turn unless an active card states otherwise. See The Aether Pouch, Section 6.

## Crafting

The Player may sacrifice 3 matching Cards of the same type (Troop, Aether, Equipment, or Hero) to randomly receive 1 Card of that type one Level higher. Likewise, 3 sacrificed Chips reward 1 Chip of the same type one Rarity higher.

## Consumables

An equipped Consumable Card is always available during combat and can be used at any time cards may be played. Once used, it returns to its respective Loot pile. Only 3 types of Consumable Cards may be brought to each battle; any others remain in the Player's Inventory.

**Emergency Evac Flare: **A carried item that triggers the early-departure rule described under Emergency Exit in §Battlefield Tile Types. The Flare is how you leave without reaching an Exit tile.

**Rapid Recovery Stim: **Instantly heals a target friendly Unit on the Battlefield to maximum HP.

**Grenade: **Deals 50% of a non-Boss Unit's maximum HP as Damage.

**Healing Item: **A category of Consumable that heals a Unit or Units by X amount.

**Medical Supplies: **Every Troop is mechanical since Revision 15.5, so Medical Supplies no longer return a Troop. Their role is an open question.

**Repair Kit: **Returns an Incapacitated Troop's Troop Card from the Discard Pile to the Player's hand; the card must then be replayed at its normal cost. Every Troop is mechanical, so a Repair Kit serves any Troop. It cannot target Heroes. On a card this is written with the **Recover** keyword (Revision 15.7).


Consumables may be selected or swapped from the Deployment Inventory between POIs, but never while inside one. All Consumables are lost in a Deployment Wipe.

# 10. The Hangar

The Hangar is the Player's home base between deployments. Heroes are selected, Decks are managed, items are crafted, bounties and quests are turned in, and consumables, cards, and Chips are bought and sold here.

**Hangar Inventory: **Each Player's personal, unlimited storage. Contents are not lost when a Strike Team is wiped out.

Party Management: Before exploring KETER, the Player assembles a Strike Team: up to 3 Heroes, one shared Deck, and the Action Cards assigned to each Hero. A deployment brings one Strike Team. Heroes beyond a Floor's capacity are held in reserve.

Resupply: no longer required. All Units recover automatically at the end of every Combat, so nothing needs reviving on return to the Hangar. The Surgery Bot has been retired.

**Bounty Board: **Lists 2 Enemies and 1 Boss/Mini-Boss. Turning in a matching bounty doubles its Shard reward and unlocks the Quartermaster Bot's Secret Supply until the next rotation.

**The Shop: **Available while in the Hangar. Each restock draws 4 Neutral Loot Cards plus 2 Loot Cards from each District whose Black Lab has been cleared, matching the Player's level.

**The Secret Supply: **Contains 3 items from the Loot Pile 1 level above the Player. The Player may choose 1 card from each District whose Black Lab has been cleared; these persist until the next redraw.

**Recruiting Heroes ****&**** Troops: **There is no separate Recruitment Roster. Hero and Troop Cards are mixed into the general Loot pool for their Level and District, and are acquired through normal Loot draws or the Reveal Rule.

**Determining Inventory Stock: **As long as the Player has explored at least 1 previously unknown tile, the Bounty Board and Shop are shuffled and redrawn.

An 8.5×11 inch hard tablet that tracks key information throughout a playthrough.

## Inventory

There are two Inventories. The Hangar Inventory is saved between all deployments and is only accessible while in the Hangar. The Deployment Inventory holds everything collected during the current deployment. Loot enters it automatically, but it is access-locked: Decks cannot be modified from it, and its contents cannot be used until the Strike Team returns to the Hangar. The Deployment Inventory is wiped entirely if a deployment ends in a wipe.

**Total Power Score: **Sum of the power score of all Item, Hero, and Troop Cards.

**Roster: **The Strike Team being brought on a deployment: up to 3 Heroes.

**Strike Team Roster: **Current Strike Team and the maximum Unit Slots available, as set by its Hero's Leadership.

**Deck: **Separate from the Deployment Inventory; used specifically in Combat. A Strike Team has one Deck of 10 to 60 cards, shared by all of its Heroes.

**Total Affinities: **Sum of all Aether Chips of a given District Affinity. Higher Affinity unlocks access to higher-Skull-Level POIs.

## The Corner Cluster

One element occupies the top-right of every card, and which one depends on the type.

- **Affinity pips (Heroes) — **one pip per Affinity Level. Levels are capped at 6 in total across both Affinities, matching the Player Level ceiling; raising one lowers the other.

- **Skulls (Enemy, Mini-Boss, Boss) — **Skull Level 1–6, drawn in the Affinity colour. A Boss carries the Skull Level of its POI.

- **Cost pips (Combat Cards) — **read left to right like a Magic: The Gathering mana cost. The neutral numeral pip is generic Aether payable from any Chip; each coloured pip is one unit of that District's Aether.

- **Affinity pip (Consumable, Prototype) — **a single hex, drawn only if the item belongs to a District. Generic items need none.

## Who May Use a Card

Nothing restricts who may use a card (Revision 15.8). Any Hero may be assigned any Action Card, any Unit may use any Equipment, and any Troop may perform any Command. A card's Affinity sets only which Aether pays for it, and its Combat Role only describes the kind of effect it has.

Usability Restrictions, which let an Equipment, Action or Command Card name a subtype or Archetypes that alone could use it, were removed in Revision 15.8. Those cards no longer carry Archetypes: an Archetype is a label other cards target a Unit by, and only Units have them.

- **Action Cards — **print their use cost: Energy, Aether, or both (Section 3).

Individual effects may also carry their own Combat Role, describing the kind of effect each one is.

## Card Levels & Scaling

Every card carries a Level from 1 to 6, matching the Player Level ceiling. Loot generally matches Player Level, though higher-Level cards may be used regardless of the Player's own Level.

Scaling is baked rather than derived: values are written onto the card when it is scaled, so what is printed is what applies at the table. A card's Level 1 values are kept as a base snapshot, and every scaling operation recomputes from that base, so multipliers can never compound.

| **Scaling Rule** | **L1** | **L2** | **L3** | **L4** | **L5** | **L6** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HP and Action Damage | ×1.00 | ×1.35 | ×1.70 | ×2.05 | ×2.40 | ×2.75 |
| Everything else | frozen | frozen | frozen | frozen | frozen | frozen |

Health Points and Action Damage follow the curve; Armor Class, Integrity, Movement Speed, Attack Range, Unit Slots, Action Slots, and NRG per turn are all frozen by default. Rounding is to the nearest whole number, and ∞ passes through untouched.

Damage scales from each effect's own Damage value. Damage written into rules prose will not scale, which is the practical reason Damage is recorded per effect rather than as a Unit stat.

## Card Numbering

Cards are numbered D[District]-[Type code][number] — D3-H01 is District 3, Hero 01. Each type keeps its own sequence, so D3-H01 and D3-E01 coexist.
